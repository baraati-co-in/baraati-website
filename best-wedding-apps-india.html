Best wedding apps in India: which one you actually need.

There is no single best wedding app, because “wedding app” describes four different products doing four different jobs. Here is how to tell which one you are looking for.

Last updated 2 August 2026 · Written by Baraati LLP, Pune

Disclosure, up front. This guide is published by Baraati LLP, which makes Baraati — one of the products discussed below. We have written it by job-to-be-done rather than as a ranking, we have not invented features or prices for anyone else, and where a competitor is the better answer for your situation we say so. For current features and pricing, always check each company's own site.

The short answer

Four jobs, four categories. Most Indian couples use two of them at different stages of the same wedding.

The two most commonly confused categories are the first and the third. A marketplace helps you decide; a guest app helps you run the thing you decided on. Using a marketplace does not give your 300 guests a schedule, and using a guest app will not find you a decorator.

1. Vendor marketplaces — for the booking phase

These are directories: vendors list themselves with photos, packages and reviews, and you browse, shortlist and enquire. They also publish enormous libraries of real-wedding photos and editorial content, which is where most Indian couples start for inspiration. Several bundle free planning tools — a 12-month checklist, a budget calculator, a guest list — alongside the directory.

  • WedMeGood — the largest Indian wedding marketplace, with vendor listings across roughly twenty categories, a very large real-wedding photo library, editorial content, and free checklist, budget and guest-list tools. Available on iOS and Android.
  • WeddingWire India and ShaadiSaga — vendor discovery with reviews and enquiry flows.
  • WeddingBazaar — vendor services, generally more price-led.

Use one when: you are three to twelve months out and still choosing suppliers. Their limit: their business model is vendor lead generation, so the guest-facing side of your wedding — the invitation everyone opens, the RSVP you can count, the schedule 300 people follow across five days — is not what they are built to carry.

2. Guest-experience apps — for the wedding itself

This is the category that barely existed in India five years ago and is now the fastest-growing part of wedding technology. One private app is created per wedding. Guests join it, and it holds everything they need: the invitation, their RSVP, the ceremony-by-ceremony schedule, venue maps, travel details, room allocations, the photo feed and, increasingly, games.

Joy and Appy Couple

The established international equivalents. Joy (withjoy.com) pairs a free wedding website with a guest app and RSVP, and is strong at what it does. Appy Couple is a paid premium wedding app and website. Both are built around the Western single-day model — one ceremony, one reception, a registry — so the Indian pieces (a five-ceremony schedule, hotel-room allocation, guest travel across many arrivals, ID collection) are not part of their design. If your wedding is a single-day event with a registry, they are a reasonable fit.

3. Wedding website builders — mostly a US answer

Zola, The Knot and Joy dominate this category in the United States, where the wedding website plus gift registry is the cultural norm. In India the registry convention is much weaker, and a public wedding website sits awkwardly against how private most families want the details of a wedding to stay. That is why Baraati's equivalent — the published album on the Premium plan — appears after the wedding rather than before it: it is a record to share, not an invitation to broadcast.

4. The one everyone actually uses: WhatsApp

Realistically, the default competitor is not an app at all. It is four WhatsApp groups, a PDF invitation, a Google Sheet and a lot of phone calls. It is free and everyone already has it, and for a wedding of fifty people it mostly works.

It stops working in five specific ways, and they are worth naming because they are the actual reason this category exists:

  1. No countable RSVP. “Will attend 🙏” between forty “Congratulations!!” messages is not a number you can give a caterer.
  2. Updates get buried. A venue change posted at 11 PM is gone by morning.
  3. Privacy. A 300-person group exposes every guest's phone number to every other guest.
  4. Photos scatter. They end up across hundreds of camera rolls and are never collected.
  5. It cannot model the structure. Five ceremonies across four days, each with a different subset of guests, is not something a chat thread can hold.
