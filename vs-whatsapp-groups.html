Why the group chat breaks

WhatsApp was built for chatting. Not for a wedding.

It's free and everyone has it — which is exactly why weddings default to it, and exactly why it turns into chaos.

📵

Every guest's number is exposed to hundreds of strangers the moment they're added. There's no taking that back.

Questions

The switch, answered.

What is a good alternative to a wedding WhatsApp group?

Baraati is a private, invite-only wedding app that replaces the group of WhatsApp groups. It keeps the invitation, RSVP, schedule, travel, hotel details, photos and games in one place, and no guest's phone number is exposed to everyone else.

Why are WhatsApp groups a problem for weddings?

Every guest's number is visible to hundreds of strangers, RSVPs get lost in a scroll of congratulations, important updates disappear, photos are scattered across chats, and there's no single source of truth for the schedule.

Do my guests need to learn a complicated app?

No. Guests tap one invite link, sign in with a one-time code — no password — and land on your invitation. It's designed for ages 8 to 80 with no onboarding. More on the guest app →

Is Baraati private?

Yes. Baraati is invite-only, has no ads, no location tracking and no behavioural analytics, and doesn't sell your data. Government ID documents, when collected, are encrypted and auto-deleted after the wedding. How it's secured →
